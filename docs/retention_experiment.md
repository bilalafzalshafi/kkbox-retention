# Proposed randomized retention experiment

Status: design proposal. No outreach has occurred and no retention effect has been measured.

The model identifies subscribers at elevated risk; a high predicted probability does not tell us whether contacting them will help. After validating the model on real data, freeze its features, fitted parameters, monthly eligibility rules and top-10% threshold policy. Apply contactability, existing opt-outs, and conflicting-campaign exclusions before ranking the eligible contactable population; rerun capacity calculations for that population. These fields are not available in the KKBOX extract and are requirements for a live experiment.

Within the highest-risk tenth of this population, randomize subscriber IDs 1:1 to a single supportive renewal reminder with a direct account-management link, or to usual service with no extra retention reminder. Avoid discounting in the initial test so that the treatment is interpretable. Send within 24 hours of monthly assignment. Standard service notifications continue identically in both arms. Do not send anything until an operator has approved and launched the experiment in the actual product.

Stratify by calendar month, auto-renew status and risk band within the selected audience. Assign a subscriber once across the entire experiment and preserve that assignment; do not rerandomize customers when they recur. Maintain a registry so repeat eligibility cannot create cross-arm exposure. Analysis uses each subscriber's first randomized snapshot and the scheduled expiry that made the subscriber eligible at assignment. Log assignment, attempted send, delivery, opt-out and subsequent billing events. Delivery is a process metric; primary analysis retains everyone randomized, including failed deliveries.

The primary endpoint is renewal with gap less than 30 days, using the same cancellation-shortening and same-day ordering rules as the observational project. The renewal clock starts from the scheduled expiry recorded at randomization, but later cancellation records can shorten the adjusted expiry before the first later non-cancellation transaction. A gap of 30 days is churn. Wait until all randomized users have complete follow-up before the planned analysis. Estimate the treatment-minus-control difference in renewal probability under intention to treat, with a two-sided 95% confidence interval and a 5% test. Use prespecified stratification-adjusted estimation as the primary estimator, with the unadjusted difference as a check. Prespecify how transaction ambiguities are adjudicated using product billing records; do not exclude users after randomization based on post-treatment data quality. If outcomes remain missing, report missingness by arm and sensitivity bounds.

Secondary outcomes are net subscription revenue and sustained paid subscription at 60 days after original expiry. Guardrails are opt-out rate, complaint rate and support contacts per assigned subscriber. Report extra reminders per incremental renewal and net value after contact costs. Avoid interpreting a high model capture rate as an experimental effect size. A message that accelerates an already-planned renewal can improve the short-window endpoint without increasing long-term revenue, which is why the 60-day endpoint matters.

Choose the minimum worthwhile improvement from unit economics before launch. Illustrative planning values are a 70% control renewal rate and an absolute 3-percentage-point improvement, two-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power. The ordinary two-proportion normal approximation yields approximately 3,554 subscribers per arm (7,108 total), before adjustments for missing outcomes or delivery constraints. These are assumptions, not KKBOX estimates. Replace the baseline with a real, earlier-period estimate for the selected audience, and recalculate using the included power function. Randomize enough subscribers under intention to treat; do not inflate an expected per-delivered effect into a per-assigned effect.

Run to the prespecified sample size, await complete follow-up, and analyze once. If sequential monitoring is necessary, select a sequential design before launch. Stop for clear harm using prespecified operational rules. The initial experiment estimates the effect within the high-risk audience. It does not establish that high-risk targeting is better than random targeting across all subscribers; that requires an additional randomized audience-policy comparison.

Launch broadly only if the estimated effect and its uncertainty support positive incremental value while guardrails remain acceptable. An inconclusive result supports further evidence collection, not a claim of no effect. A harmful or uneconomic result is a reason to revise or stop the intervention even when churn prediction is excellent.
